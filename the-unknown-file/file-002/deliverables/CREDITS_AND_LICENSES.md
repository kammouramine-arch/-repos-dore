## 2. Sound effects — original, procedural

All sound effects were synthesised for this episode by `tools/sfx.py` from noise and short decaying resonances. No sample libraries, no third-party recordings, and no AI audio were used.

Sound palette:
- suit-fan air, helmet breathing and water bubbles/slosh;
- restrained radio static with dropouts, and 250 ms comm-key beeps;
- tether click, glove and handrail contacts, hatch thump, latch, repress hiss;
- technical ticks, low impacts, air transitions, paper and stamp.

**There is no music of any kind:** no score, no drones, no melodic or rhythmic content, and no intro or outro music. The cue sheets are `tools/cues_short.py` and `tools/cues_long.py`.

Rights: original work of the production, owned by the channel.

## 5. Research sources (facts, not on-screen media)

- NASA Mishap Investigation Board, *ISS EVA Suit Water Intrusion — High Visibility Close Call*, IRIS S-2013-199-00005, Dec 20, 2013. [NTRS 20140016950](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20140016950).
  - Volumes: ~0.5–1 L found after EVA 22; an estimated 1–1.5 L during EVA 23.
  - Cause and findings: the drink-bag misdiagnosis; contamination blocking the separator drum holes.
- NASA, ICES-2015 failure analysis of the EMU fan/pump/separator ([NTRS 20150003027](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20150003027)). Source for the inorganic silica/silicate deposits.
- NASA History, *10 Years Ago: EVA 23 — How a High Visibility Close Call Cut Short a Spacewalk* (2023). Source for the timeline: water reported ~44 min in; helmet off ~100 min after the start.
