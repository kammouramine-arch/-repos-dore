|---|---|---|

| Almost completely surrounded by Senegal, with one opening to the Atlantic | Established geography | Natural Earth / geoBoundaries |
| Communities lived and traded along the river before colonial borders | Established, shown generically | General history. **No kingdom borders or settlement names are invented**: the markers are labelled *illustrative*. |
| James Island: the English held the fort from 1661 | Established | World Monuments Fund, *James Island*: settled in 1651 by Courlanders, taken by the English in 1661 and renamed Fort James |
| Bathurst (today Banjul), 1816 | Established | The British purchased St Mary's Island in 1816, and Alexander Grant founded Bathurst there (Wikipedia, *Alexander Grant (British Army officer)*; *Banjul*) |
| MacCarthy Island, 1823 | Established | Britannica, *MacCarthy Island*: ceded to Captain Alexander Grant in 1823 |
| France expanded colonial control across Senegal | Established | Shown as a **soft-edged, approximate** zone with no fixed border before 1889 (on-screen note: "Shading is approximate — no fixed border yet") |
| 1889 Anglo-French agreement, signed in Paris on 10 August 1889 | Established | UK Parliament treaty record: *Arrangement between Great Britain and France respecting the Delimitation of the English and French Possessions on the West Coast of Africa* (Paris, 10 Aug 1889); Wikipedia, *Anglo-French Convention of 1889* |
| Boundary roughly 10 km from the river on either side, as far as Yarbutenda, with an arc around Yarbutenda in the east | Established | [Hansard, 1 June 1904, *Anglo-French Convention Bill*](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1904/jun/01/the-anglo-french-convention-bill) (under the 1889 arrangement "a strip, ten kilometres wide on each bank of the lower portion of the river, was reserved for Great Britain"); Wikipedia, *Anglo-French Convention of 1889* and *The Gambia–Senegal border* (straight segments in the west, about 10 km from the river inland, a 10 km arc at Yarbutenda) |
| Delimitation commission 1890–91; pillars; further demarcation in 1911 and 1925 | Established | Wikipedia, *Anglo-French Convention of 1889* / *The Gambia–Senegal border* (Anglo-French Commission of Delimitation, Dec 1890 – Apr 1891; demarcation 1911 and 1925) |
| The cannonball legend | **Labelled as a legend, not verified history** | No reliable evidence. Shown as an illustration, stamped LEGEND — NOT VERIFIED HISTORY and then NO RELIABLE EVIDENCE. |

**Maps follow the dates shown:**
- Before 1889, no Senegal or Gambia borders are drawn. French control is a soft zone, and British posts are point markers.
- The crisp British Gambia corridor appears only from the 1889 agreement onward.
- Modern Senegal and The Gambia, with their modern colours and labels, appear only in "today" scenes and from independence in 1965 onward.
