# THE UNKNOWN FILE — Business model

## The funnel

```
CONTENT  →  CURIOSITY  →  WEBSITE  →  FREE EXPERIENCE  →  PAID CASE  →  MORE CASES
```

Short-form video is the only acquisition channel that matters at the start.
The website exists to convert, and the free case is where the conversion
actually happens. Every bio link points at the free case, never at checkout.

## Why the free case is the whole strategy

A stranger will not pay €14.99 for "an interactive mystery" because they
have no idea what that means. Nobody does — the category barely exists.

Fifteen minutes inside a real case solves that completely. It demonstrates
the format, teaches the one skill the paid case is built around, and ends on
a line that makes the next file feel necessary. It costs nothing to give
away and it is the single highest-leverage asset in the business.

**If the free case does not hold a stranger for fifteen minutes, no amount of
copy on the product page will sell them the long one.** That is the metric
to optimise before any other.

## Pricing

| Product | Price | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Case #000 — free sample | €0 | Acquisition. Never gated, never emailed for. |
| Case #001 — single case | **€14.99** | Two to three hours of designed experience. Sits above an ebook, below a boxed escape room (€25–35), which is exactly where the perceived value is. |
| The Founding Files — 3 cases | **€34.99** | €11.66 a case. Saves €9.98. Converts a buyer into a subscriber-shaped customer before a subscription exists. |
| Archive Access — membership | **€7.99/mo** | Withheld until six cases are published. Selling a subscription to three files is a bad deal and would be indefensible. |

**On €14.99 rather than €9.99.** The lower price does not double conversion
in this category, and it signals "ebook". The product is closer in shape to a
boxed puzzle than to a download, and it should be priced where that
comparison is flattering rather than confusing.

**The bundle is the real product.** It doubles average order value at
essentially zero marginal cost and pre-sells the next two releases, which is
what makes a release schedule survivable.

## Unit economics

Digital goods, so marginal cost is a rounding error. The real costs are
**writing time** and **payment fees**.

- A case takes serious writing. That is the constraint on everything else.
- Merchant of record (Lemon Squeezy / Paddle) takes roughly 5% + fixed fee
  and removes EU VAT registration entirely. On a €14.99 product with no team,
  that is worth more than the points saved on Stripe.
- Hosting is effectively free at this scale — the site is static.

## Roadmap

**Phase 1 — prove the format (now).**
Case #001 live. Free case live. Payment connected. One channel, posting
consistently. Success is measured on free-case completion rate, not revenue.

**Phase 2 — a schedule people can rely on.**
Cases #002 and #003 shipped to Founding Files buyers. A release cadence the
writing can actually sustain. Bundle becomes the default offer.

**Phase 3 — the archive as a product.**
Six published cases opens Archive Access at €7.99/month. Accounts arrive
here, because now there is a reason to have one.

**Phase 4 — the format expands.**
Longer cases across multiple sittings. Cases designed for two people
investigating together. A case that unlocks in stages over a week.

## What would make this fail

Being honest about this is more useful than a projection.

1. **Cases that are not really solvable.** The moment a reveal depends on
   something the player was never shown, the product becomes a story with a
   twist and the price stops making sense. This is the one that kills it.
2. **A release schedule that outruns the writing.** Every shipped case must
   be as tight as #001. Slower is survivable; thin is not.
3. **Blurring fiction and real cases.** The brand's entire credibility is
   the labelling rule. Break it once for reach and both categories are worth
   less permanently.
4. **Selling before the free case is good.** Traffic on a weak funnel is
   spent traffic. Fix the middle of the funnel first.
5. **Building an account system nobody asked for.** Friction at the exact
   moment a stranger has decided to try something.

## The numbers to watch

Only three, in this order:

1. **Free-case starts per 100 sessions** — is the site explaining the product?
2. **Free-case completions per start** — is the product good?
3. **Paid conversions per free-case completion** — is the price right?

A weak (2) cannot be fixed by improving (1) or (3), and improving (1) while
(2) is weak just spends traffic faster.
