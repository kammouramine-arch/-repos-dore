# THE UNKNOWN FILE — Content Library

Thirty-six short-form concepts, categorised before they are written.

Every concept carries one of two labels, and the label is decided **before**
the script exists. Deciding afterwards is how brands in this space end up
inventing quotes.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **FICTIONAL CASE / THE UNKNOWN FILE** | Written by us. Every name invented. Labelled fiction on screen and in the caption. Leads to a file you can investigate. |
| **REAL CASE / DOCUMENTED** | Has a public documentary record. No invented detail, no invented quote, no invented witness. Sources named on screen and in the caption. Never sold, never converted into a paid product. |

### Non-negotiables for documented cases

1. **Source before you script.** Every factual claim traces to a named,
   checkable source. If you cannot source it, it does not go in — however
   good it sounds.
2. **Contested is stated as contested.** Where accounts differ, say so.
   Never pick the more dramatic version and present it as settled.
3. **No accusation.** Not by name, not by implication, not by editing,
   juxtaposition or omission. Living people are not suspects on our channel.
4. **Folklore is labelled folklore.** Several famous "unexplained" stories
   have no documentary basis at all. Those get a third framing —
   *documented as a story, not as an event* — or they get dropped.
5. **A family's request wins.** If someone connected to a case asks us to
   take something down, it comes down first and gets discussed after.

## B · Documented real cases — sourced, never sold

Angles only. **Research and source every claim before scripting.** The
"before you publish" column is the specific thing that most often gets
repeated wrongly online about that case — check it first.

| # | Case | Angle | Before you publish |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Mary Celeste (1872) | What the boarding crew actually recorded finding aboard, versus the version that entered folklore through fiction. | Most "famous details" come from a Conan Doyle short story, not the salvage record. Separate the two explicitly. |
| B2 | The Somerton Man (1948) | A case where the physical evidence is unusually well documented and the identification took seventy years. | A 2022 identification has been proposed by researchers; report its status accurately rather than as settled or as unsolved. |
| B3 | The Wow! signal (1977) | What the printout actually shows, and why a single non-repeating narrowband event is hard to interpret either way. | Do not state the origin. Competing natural explanations exist and are contested. |
| B4 | Voynich manuscript | Why the statistical structure of the text is the interesting part, rather than the illustrations. | Numerous "solutions" have been announced and none is accepted. Say so. |
| B5 | Dyatlov Pass (1959) | How the investigation record reads, and what the 2019–2021 reassessment proposed. | Name the proposed mechanism as a proposal, not a conclusion. Avoid the sensational injury claims that circulate without source. |
| B6 | Flight 19 (1945) | The radio log and the weather, as recorded — a navigation failure story rather than a triangle story. | The "Bermuda Triangle" framing postdates the event by decades. |
| B7 | Max Headroom broadcast intrusion (1987) | A televised event with surviving footage and a case that was never closed. | Never name or speculate about identities. Several innocent people have been publicly accused online. |

| B11 | Roanoke (1587) | What the colony record actually contains, and what the archaeology has since suggested. | "Vanished without a trace" is not what the record says. |
| B12 | The Antikythera mechanism | An object that was genuinely inexplicable for decades and is now largely understood — a case of a mystery that ended. | Present as solved. It is a useful counterweight to the format. |
| B13 | UVB-76 / "The Buzzer" | A signal that has been transmitting for decades and is monitored by hobbyists worldwide. | Attribution and purpose are unconfirmed. State that plainly. |
| B14 | Kryptos, sculpture at CIA HQ | Three of four passages solved; the fourth remains open with published clues. | Quote the released clues accurately. |
| B15 | The Tamám Shud slip | How a torn scrap of a book became the anchor of an entire investigation. | Overlaps B2. Keep the two clips distinct. |
| B16 | The Zodiac ciphers | The 340 cipher was broken in 2020 by three amateurs. What it said, and what it did not say. | It did not identify anyone. Say so in the clip. |
| B17 | Abandoned: the Salton Sea | A place that was built, sold, and left — documented decline rather than mystery. | Frame as documented history, not as a disappearance. |
| B18 | Abandoned: Hashima Island | An entire island city, documented in detail before and after. | Photographs must be your own or properly licensed. |

| B20 | The man from Taured | Frequently retold as fact; the documentary trail runs out almost immediately. | **Label folklore**, and say explicitly that we could not source it. That clip is more interesting than the myth. |

## C · Format pieces — teaching the audience to read a file

These convert. A viewer who has learned to check a timestamp is a viewer
who wants a file to check.

| # | Concept | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | "Check the clock" | Sixty seconds on why a timestamp is a claim, not a fact. Uses the free case's tide deduction. |
| C2 | "What a red herring actually is" | A red herring is not a lie. It is a true, irrelevant contradiction. Two examples, one of each. |
| C3 | "Three exhibits, one contradiction" | Put three documents on screen. Two agree. Ask which. |
| C4 | "The detail nobody checked" | A famous line about a case turns out to be a paraphrase of a paraphrase. |
| C5 | "Instruments have no motive" | Why the machine record beats the human record, and the one situation where it does not. |
| C6 | "Would you have noticed?" | Ten seconds of a document. Two seconds of blank screen. The answer next post. |
| C7 | Evidence breakdown | One exhibit, forty seconds, annotated on screen. |
| C8 | Hidden clue challenge | A frame containing one inconsistency. Comments find it. No prize, no gimmick. |
| C9 | "How we build a case" | Backwards, from the solution. Thirty seconds of process, which is itself a trust signal. |
| C10 | "Why we don't sell real cases" | Say the policy out loud. It costs one video and buys the whole brand's credibility. |

## Posting rhythm

A workable weekly slate, adjustable to whatever the writing can sustain:

- **4 × fictional teasers** — the engine. Each ends on the invitation, not the answer.
- **2 × documented cases** — the authority. Sourced on screen.
- **1 × format piece** — the conversion. Teaches the skill the product sells.

Every clip's caption carries its label. Every bio link points to the **free
case**, never straight to checkout: a viewer who has never bought anything
gets fifteen minutes of the real product before we ask for anything.

## The five beats

1. **The frame** — a place, a date, a time. No adjectives.
2. **The facts** — three of them, one per line, flat.
3. **The break** — the fourth fact, which cannot coexist with the first three.
4. **The hold** — one second of nothing.
5. **The invitation** — "Would you have noticed?" then the file.

## Voice

Calm. Intelligent. Cinematic. Serious. Minimal.

Never: "OMG", "you won't believe", "the most terrifying thing ever",
emoji in the script, a reaction shot, a music sting on the reveal.

Always: short sentences, exact numbers, and the confidence to stop talking.
