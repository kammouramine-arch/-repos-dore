## THE DESIGN PROBLEM

Four constraints, and they conflict:

1. **It must read as premium on a catalogue blank.** No proprietary garment, no custom
   trim. Perceived value has to come from the mark and the system around it.
2. **It must survive 45mm embroidery and DTG on textured cotton.** This kills hairlines,
   ultra-light weights, tight counters and fine detail. Most "quiet luxury" identities
   fail here.
3. **It must be globally legible and culturally unbounded** — France, Europe, UK, US,
   Middle East, Asia. No national reference, no religious form, no idiom.
4. **The wordmark must work with no symbol at all.**

Constraint 2 is the one that does the most work. It eliminates the entire family of
delicate, wide-tracked, hairline identities that dominate premium fashion — they look
correct on a screen and disappear on a garment. **We are designing for thread and ink
first, screen second.** That single inversion is what separates this from the default.

# 1–4. THE MARKS

## Three territories explored

Three genuinely different strategic positions, not three styles of one idea.

### TERRITORY 01 — THE ARCHIVE
*Institutional record-keeping. Museum object labels, technical plates, index systems.*

Light-to-regular grotesque, wide tracking, identity carried by information design —
codes, indices, specification tables — rather than by any mark.

**For:** maximum restraint, ages extremely well, costs almost nothing to execute, and
it aligns with the spec-honesty pillar already locked in Phase 01.

**Against — and it is disqualifying:** wide-tracked light type is structurally weak on
its own, so it **fails the requirement that the wordmark work without a symbol**. It also
fails constraint 2 — light weights and open tracking are exactly what dies in embroidery
and on cotton. And it is a well-populated look; a great deal of premium fashion already
lives here.

**Verdict: rejected.** Correct instincts, wrong physics.

### TERRITORY 02 — THE DATUM
*Everything sits on a line. The line is where you start.*

Derived from the brand's own locked chapter line — **BEGIN AT GROUND LEVEL** — rather
than from a borrowed aesthetic. A single horizontal governs the wordmark, the layouts,
the labels and the packaging. The wordmark's own horizontal events are aligned to one
shared height, giving the word a visible internal register.

**For:** it comes from language we already own, so it cannot be lifted wholesale by a
competitor without also lifting the words. It carries real weight, so it survives thread
and ink. It is orthogonal and abstract, so it is culturally neutral everywhere. And it
gives the chapter architecture a visual mechanism rather than a decoration.

**Against:** it can drift technical — an architecture practice rather than a clothing
house — if the photography and the writing do not carry warmth.

**Verdict: recommended.**

### TERRITORY 03 — THE LOAD
*Typography as mass. The product's defining specification is weight; the type expresses it.*

Heavy, tight, dense letterforms. Strength that is contained rather than shouted.

**For:** immediate physical presence, excellent on garments, strong at small sizes, and
conceptually tied to the one thing our product genuinely is — heavyweight.

**Against:** it is the closest of the three to the category norm. Heavy condensed type is
what most gym brands already do, and the distance between "powerful" and "generic
sportswear" is very short. More importantly the concept is **one note** — it produces a
wordmark and then has nothing further to say about layout, packaging, or chapters.

**Verdict: rejected as a system, absorbed as a property.** Its correct insight — that the
mark must carry visible weight — is taken into Territory 02's wordmark.

## RECOMMENDATION — TERRITORY 02, THE DATUM

> **The house is built on one horizontal line. Everything begins from it.**

The system has three elements and a strict hierarchy between them.

## 1. Primary wordmark — `GYMREIGN`

Set as **one word, uppercase, never broken**. Never `GYM REIGN`. Never `GymReign` —
camel case is a software-company tell and cheapens the word instantly.

**The construction idea.** The word contains four horizontal events: two G crossbars,
the E middle arm, and the R junction. In the production wordmark **all four are drawn to
a single shared height**, so an invisible register runs the length of the word. Nothing
announces it. It is the reason the word looks engineered rather than typed.

| Specification | Value |
|---|---|
| Base face | Archivo Semibold (600), Omnibus-Type, SIL Open Font License |
| Case | Uppercase only |
| Tracking | −0.005em to +0.01em, set optically — **not** wide-tracked |
| Weight | Medium-bold. Enough stem to survive thread; not so heavy the counters close |
| Horizontal register | All four horizontal events aligned to one height |
| Terminals | Cut flat and horizontal |

**Status: exploratory.** The specimen is *set* in the base face; the four horizontals are
close but not aligned, because a font cannot do that. **Aligning them is the production
work**, and it requires a type designer redrawing outlines. See §21.

## 2. Secondary wordmark / lockups

Four permitted lockups. No others may be created.

| Lockup | Composition | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Primary** | Wordmark alone | Default. Most applications |
| **B — House** | Wordmark, datum device beneath at wordmark width, tagline below in mono | Site footer, packaging, formal contexts |
| **C — Chapter** | Wordmark, datum device, `CHAPTER 001 — ASCENSION` in mono | Chapter contexts only. Retires with the chapter |
| **D — Stacked** | Monogram above, wordmark below, centred | Square formats only — social avatar, cap crown, favicon |

The tagline **EARN YOUR REIGN** is locked and appears only in lockup B, set in mono at
+0.2em tracking. It is never set in the wordmark's face at wordmark scale — that would
create a second wordmark competing with the first.

## 3. GR monogram

G and R **locked by one shared datum**: the G's crossbar and the R's bowl-junction sit at
exactly the same height, and the two letters are set close enough that the alignment
reads as a single horizontal event crossing both. That shared line is the same idea as
the wordmark's internal register, at two-letter scale.

Geometric construction, 14-unit stroke on a 120-unit cap height, flat terminals.

**Use where the wordmark will not fit or would be illegible:** cap front, sleeve, a
9mm woven label, favicon, social avatar. **Never alongside the wordmark in the same
field** — that is the single most common way a good identity is made to look amateur.

**Tested:** legible to 24px on screen and reverses cleanly on black.
**Production note:** the G's crossbar creates a small enclosed counter which will fill in
below roughly 20mm embroidered. Opening that counter for small-scale embroidery is a type
designer's job (§21).

## 4. The datum device — the micro-mark

**This is deliberately not a pictorial symbol.** Early exploration produced an abstract
elevation glyph; rasterised and tested, it read as the letter "C" and collapsed at small
size. It was discarded rather than defended. **The brief said the symbol should be useful
but not forced — an invented glyph that reads as the wrong letter is forced.**

What replaces it is a system device rather than a logo:

> **A single rule, interrupted once at the proportion 3:5 — the split between GYM and
> REIGN.**

It is the datum itself, carrying the word's own structure. It is used as the rule beneath
the wordmark, the divider between layout sections, the hem tab, the label edge, and the
embroidered detail on a cap side. Repeated across every surface, it becomes the
signature — which is how restrained houses are actually recognised, far more than by a
glyph.

**Production note:** below roughly 20mm the interruption closes in thread. **Below 20mm,
use the solid rule with no notch.** Both forms are correct; the notch is a detail that
rewards proximity and disappears at distance, which is exactly what a garment detail
should do.

# 5. TYPOGRAPHY SYSTEM

**Two families. No serif.**

The temptation at this point is to add an elegant serif for "luxury". That is precisely
the fake-luxury move Phase 01 §6 bans. Warmth comes from photography, paper and writing —
not from a display serif borrowed to signal expense.

| Role | Face | Licence | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | **Archivo** (400/500/600/700) | SIL OFL | Squarish grotesque skeleton, industrial without being cold, variable width axes, high legibility at small sizes, free for commercial use, globally available |
| **Data & code** | **IBM Plex Mono** (400/500/600) | SIL OFL | Article codes, measurements, labels, technical layers. Has character without reading like a code editor |

Both are open-licensed — a deliberate choice at a €2,500 budget, and both are available
in every market without a foundry licence per territory.

## Scale and tracking

| Level | Size | Weight | Tracking |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display | clamp 2.4–4.6rem | 600 | −0.04em |
| Heading | 1.5–2.2rem | 600 | −0.028em |
| Subhead | 1.0–1.2rem | 600 | −0.012em |
| Body | 1.0–1.06rem | 400 | 0 |
| Label (mono, uppercase) | 0.66–0.72rem | 600 | +0.18em |
| Caption | 0.85rem | 400 | 0 |

**Rules.** Flush left, ragged right — never justified, never centred except the wordmark
in a symmetrical lockup. Measure 62–68 characters. Uppercase only for labels and the
wordmark; never for running text. No italics anywhere in the system.

# 6. NUMERICAL TYPOGRAPHY

Numbers carry more of this brand than in most — chapter numbers, article codes,
measurements, GSM, prices. They are a designed layer, not an afterthought.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Chapter number** | Always three digits, always zero-padded. `001`, `002`, `010`. Never `1` |
| **Article code** | IBM Plex Mono, uppercase, en-dash separated: `GR–001–TS–BLK` |
| **Measurements** | **Always dual-unit, always in this order:** `71 cm / 28 in`. Non-negotiable — it is the global-sizing answer and the spec-honesty pillar in one device |
| **Fabric weight** | `240 GSM` — space before unit, unit uppercase |
| **Prices** | Display without trailing zeros (`€65`); full precision at checkout (`€65.00`) |
| **Alignment** | `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` wherever digits stack |

**The chapter numeral as a graphic element.** Set at display scale, tracking −0.04em, it
functions as a compositional anchor in campaign and social work. It may be cropped by the
frame edge, but never by more than 20% of its height, and never so the digits become
ambiguous.

## 9. Accent system

**Chapter 001 has no accent colour. None.** For a first chapter this is the strongest
available statement and it costs nothing.

The system permits **exactly one accent per chapter**, introduced from a low-chroma
family, and retired when the chapter closes. Reference family:

| Name | HEX | |
|---|---|---|
| OXIDE | `#6E3229` | Deep iron red |
| STEEL | `#3E5561` | Desaturated slate blue |
| MOSS | `#414A34` | Muted olive |

**Accent rules — binding.** Maximum 5% of any surface area. Never on the wordmark,
monogram or datum device. Never as a full background field. Never two accents at once.
Never a saturated or fluorescent tone.

**No metallics. Ever.** Gold is the fake-luxury tell the brief explicitly names. Where
richness is wanted, use *material* rather than colour: blind deboss, tonal black-on-black
print, matte against gloss. Those are craft. Gold ink is costume.

**Production colour.** No Pantone or thread references are specified here, because
specifying them from a screen would be a guess. **Colour is matched physically against
samples at Phase 04–05**, and the matched references are then recorded in this document.

# 10. SIZING AND SPACING

**Clear space** on all four sides of the wordmark = the cap height of the wordmark.
For the monogram = the stroke width × 3. Nothing enters that space — no text, no image
edge, no rule, no other mark.

| Element | Digital min | Print min | Embroidery min |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wordmark | 96px wide | 20mm wide | **45mm wide** |
| Monogram | 24px | 8mm | **20mm** (below this, open the G counter) |
| Datum device | 40px | 12mm | **20mm** — below this, drop the notch |

Embroidery minimums are specifications **to be validated physically at Phase 05 sampling**
against the chosen provider's machinery. They are informed estimates, not measurements.

---

# 11. MISUSE

Never:

- Break the word — `GYM REIGN`
- Camel-case it — `GymReign`
- Set it in any face other than the drawn wordmark
- Outline, gradient, emboss, drop-shadow, glow or bevel it
- Rotate it, arc it, or set it on a curve
- Stretch or condense it non-optically
- Recolour it outside the palette, or in any metallic
- Place the monogram and the wordmark together in the same field
- Place it over busy photography without a solid field or a controlled scrim
- Create a lockup that is not one of the four in §2
- Add a strapline, city name, established-date or flag to any lockup
- Enclose it in a badge, shield, crest, circle or roundel

# 12. GRAPHIC LANGUAGE

**The permitted vocabulary is small on purpose.**

- **The datum** — one horizontal rule, present in every composition
- **The datum device** — the notched rule (§4)
- **Article codes** — `GR–001–TS–BLK`
- **Chapter numerals** — `001` at any scale
- **Orthogonal rule work** — horizontal and vertical only

## The binding constraint

> **No diagonals anywhere in the graphic system.**

Everything is horizontal or vertical. The only diagonals permitted in the entire identity
are those inside letterforms — the Y, the R's leg, the N. One rule, applied without
exception, produces more coherence than any amount of styling.

## Banned outright

Crowns · lions · wings · flames · dumbbells · barbells · shields · crests · laurels ·
mountains · summits · sunrises · ascending staircases · Latin mottoes · Greek keys ·
gothic lettering · distressed or grunge texture · speed lines · gradients · lens flare ·
drop shadows · rounded corners anywhere in the system · outline type · text on a curve ·
before-and-after constructions · any national flag or map

---

# 13. LAYOUT AND GRID

**Base unit 8px.** All spacing is a multiple.

**Web:** 12 columns, 24px gutter, 1440px maximum, text measure 62–68ch.
**Print / campaign:** 8 columns, margin never below 1/12 of the shortest edge.

**Every composition declares one datum** — a single horizontal that other elements sit on,
hang from, or align to. It may be visible as a rule or purely structural. If a layout has
no datum, it is not in the system.

**Sharp corners everywhere.** No rounded corners on any container, button, card, image or
input. This is architectural and it is absolute.

# 14. PHOTOGRAPHY

Extends the Phase 01 brief; those rules stand.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Register** | Concrete, stairwells, loading bays, underground car parks, empty gyms before opening |
| **Light** | Low ambient, one hard source. Underexposed. Deep blacks, held, not lifted |
| **Grade** | Cold neutral, warm skin retained. No teal-orange |
| **Lenses** | 35mm environment · 50mm figure · 85mm and macro for detail |
| **Subject** | Architecture over anatomy. Silhouette and detail — hands, forearms, nape, chalk, a bar |
| **Faces** | **None in Chapter 001** |
| **Motion** | None. Still and deliberate. No action shots, no blur, no smiling |
| **Ratios** | 4:5 product · 3:2 editorial · 9:16 social motion |

**Geographic neutrality is a hard requirement.** Concrete, low light and architecture read
as anywhere. A recognisable Paris backdrop reads as French and breaks §0 of the strategy.

**Detail photography is not optional.** Collar, seam, label, print edge, stitch density at
macro range. It is the single most effective signal that separates premium from
print-on-demand, and it costs nothing but time.

# 15. PRODUCT BRANDING APPLICATION

Placement rules only. **Product development is Phase 03 and has not begun.**

> **Never more than two brand appearances on a single garment.** One is usually better.

| Article | Primary | Secondary | Never |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T-shirt** | Left chest, wordmark or monogram, 45–55mm | Nape inside label | Full-chest logo. Back print over 120mm |
| **Hoodie** | Left chest, 50–60mm | Nape label, or datum device at hem | Sleeve wordmark plus chest plus back |
| **Sweatpants** | Upper-left thigh, monogram, 40mm | Inside waistband label | Leg-length wordmark |
| **Cap** | Front, embroidered monogram 45mm or wordmark 55mm | Datum device on the rear closure | Side and front and rear together |

**Embroidery over print wherever the article allows it.** Embroidery is the single most
effective anti-POD signal available — there is no print to crack, and customers associate
it with real manufacture. On the cap it is mandatory.

# 17. WEBSITE VISUAL DIRECTION

Direction only. **The site is not being built.**

> **Bone ground, dark imagery.** The category is uniformly black-on-black. The quiet one
> is light.

This is a deliberate differentiation call and it also serves the product: dark, cinematic,
underexposed photography sings against a warm light ground and muddies against a dark one.

Sharp corners · no shadows · rules instead of cards · the datum device as section divider ·
generous margins · full-bleed imagery against tight-measure text · motion limited to
opacity and small translations, all disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

**Architected for the global mandate from the first line of markup:** English-first,
measurements in cm and inches, country and region support, currency display, and a
shipping architecture that accepts a new region without restructuring. Designed in — not
launched all at once.

# 18. SOCIAL VISUAL DIRECTION

4:5 primary · 9:16 motion · 1:1 avatar carrying lockup D.

**The grid is a sequence, not a mosaic.** No nine-square puzzle layouts. Each post stands
alone and the column reads as an accumulating record.

Typography on image: mono labels only, bottom-left, at the datum. Never centred, never
large display type over a photograph. The chapter numeral is the recurring anchor.

No faces in Chapter 001 · no memes · no reposted motivational quotes · no text-on-black
quote cards · no countdown graphics.

# 19. CHAPTER 001 — VISUAL RELATIONSHIP

**The house identity never changes between chapters.** A chapter adds exactly two things:

1. The chapter numeral — `001`
2. The chapter line — `BEGIN AT GROUND LEVEL.` *(retires when the chapter closes)*

## The Chapter 001 compositional device

> **The subject sits on the datum, low in the frame. The space above it is empty.**

That empty space is the ascent not yet made. It is the chapter line expressed as
composition rather than illustrated as a picture — which is the difference between this
and the six competitors already using mountains and staircases.

It also gives the architecture somewhere to go: **the subject may rise in the frame across
future chapters.** Recorded as a Chapter 001 device that is permitted to evolve, not as a
permanent formula.

## On legal clearance

**No element in this document is cleared for use, and I am making no claim that it is.**

The word mark `GYMREIGN` still requires the professional clearance flagged in Phase 01 —
and that work is independent of this document, so **it can and should proceed in
parallel.** A figurative filing covering the drawn wordmark or the monogram can only
happen once those are actually drawn.

**The sequence:** approve a direction → word-mark clearance returns → wordmark drawn by a
designer → figurative clearance and filing → production. **Do not commission label
production, embroidery digitising or any physical branding before clearance returns.**
