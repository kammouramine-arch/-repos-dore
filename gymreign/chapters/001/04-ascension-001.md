# 1. CREATIVE CONCEPT

## 1.1 The idea

Most first collections try to look established. Ours does the opposite, because the
opposite is true and the customer can tell.

> **ASCENSION 001 is about establishing zero.**

Before anything can be claimed to have risen, there has to be a line to measure from, and
somebody has to be honest about where the line is. That is the whole chapter. Not the
climb — the **measurement taken before the climb**, which is the part nobody photographs
and everybody skips.

## 1.2 Why this is the right first chapter

It is the only honest position available to us. A house that opened this month has
ascended nothing. A first collection built on summit imagery would be the brand's first
lie, and it would be a lie in the same week we published a page promising to give
customers real numbers instead of adjectives.

So the chapter says the true thing: **we are at ground level too.** The brand and the
customer are standing on the same line, at the same time, at the beginning of the same
record. That is a genuinely uncommon thing for a new brand to admit, and admitting it is
worth more than pretending otherwise.

It also earns the number. `001` means something because we are not behaving as though it
were `014`.

## 1.3 The tension the chapter holds

`CHAPTER 001` · `ELEVATION 000`

The first chapter, at zero height. Chapter number and elevation are deliberately different
numbers, and the gap between them is the chapter's entire argument: **you have started,
and you have not yet risen. Both are true.**

**Used as an internal detail, not a headline.** `ELEVATION 000` appears on the inside
label and the packaging card — found by the owner, not announced to the street.
`CHAPTER 001` remains the public designation. This keeps the public system unambiguous
while giving the garment something that rewards attention.

*Risk noted:* two numbers can confuse if both are used publicly. That is exactly why one
of them is internal. If Phase 04 sampling shows the inside label is too small to carry it
legibly, it moves to the packaging card only.

# 3. CHAPTER VISUAL LANGUAGE

## 3.1 The governing rule

> **Chapter 001 introduces no new graphic form.**

It uses only what the house system already contains: the datum, the wordmark, the
monogram, article codes, chapter numerals, orthogonal rules. Nothing is drawn for this
chapter that is not already part of GYMREIGN.

Three reasons, and the third is the one that matters commercially:

1. **A first chapter should show the system, not decorate it.** If chapter one already
   needs a flourish, the system was not strong enough.
2. **It sets the precedent correctly.** Later chapters inflect the system; they do not
   each bring a new visual world.
3. **Type and rules are the highest-quality thing a DTG printer can produce.** Complex,
   large, multi-tone artwork is precisely where direct-to-garment printing fails — banding,
   soft edges, cracked hand-feel after washing. Flat type and hard-edged rules print
   cleanly, wash well, and hold their black.

**The aesthetic choice and the production-quality choice are the same choice.** That is
not a coincidence to be grateful for; it is the reason this direction was recommended.

## 3.2 Chapter elements

| Element | Form | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **The datum** | Solid rule, weight matched to the wordmark stem | Every garment, every layout |
| **Chapter designation** | `CHAPTER 001 — ASCENSION`, mono, +0.2em | Hoodie back, chapter page, packaging |
| **Chapter line** | `BEGIN AT GROUND LEVEL.` | Used sparingly — inside label, packaging, campaign. **Never on the outside of more than one garment** |
| **Elevation** | `ELEVATION 000` | Inside label and packaging card only |
| **Article code** | `GR–001–TS–BLK` | Inside label, product page, packaging |

## 3.3 Composition

Inherited from Phase 02 and unchanged: **the subject sits on the datum, low in the frame,
with the space above it empty.** That emptiness is the ascent not yet made — the chapter
line expressed as composition rather than illustrated as a picture.

**Still forbidden:** mountains, summits, sunrises, staircases, arrows, upward gradients,
before-and-after, and every other literal depiction of rising. Six competitors already own
that vocabulary and it is not worth contesting.

# 4. PRODUCT ARCHITECTURE

## 4.1 The problem this must solve

Four catalogue blanks, bought from a print-on-demand provider, must read as one designed
collection. Nothing about the garments themselves will do that work — the blanks are
available to everyone. **Coherence has to be engineered on top of them.**

Three layers do it. All three are required; any one alone fails.

## 4.2 Layer one — material coherence

Every garment in the chapter belongs to the same weight class and the same finish family.
No lightweight item. No mixed hand-feel. A customer who owns two pieces must feel the same
quality in both.

Requirements are set in §10 as testable targets.

## 4.3 Layer two — placement coherence: THE ELEVATION SYSTEM

**This is the mechanism that makes four blanks look like one collection.**

Every mark on every garment is positioned by **measurement from the garment's own hem** —
never "centred on the chest", never eyeballed, never left to the provider's default
placement template.

```
        ┌─────────────────┐
        │                 │
        │   ▪ mark        │   Every placement is a measured
        │                 │   elevation above the hem datum.
        │                 │
        ├─────────────────┤   ← HEM = the garment's datum, elevation 000
```

| Garment | Mark | Elevation above hem `[A — to be finalised per blank in Phase 04]` |
|---|---|---|
| T-shirt | Left chest | 380 mm |
| Hoodie | Left chest | 400 mm |
| Sweatpants | Upper-left thigh | 640 mm above the leg hem |
| Cap | Front centre | Measured from the brim edge |

Two consequences, and both matter:

- **Conceptually**, the datum stops being a graphic device and becomes a construction
  method. Everything on a GYMREIGN garment is measured from a line.
- **Practically**, it is a real production instruction. POD placement defaults vary by
  garment and by provider, and default placement is one of the clearest visual tells of
  a print-on-demand product — marks that float at slightly different heights across a
  range. **A measured spec removes that tell.** It also gives Phase 05 something objective
  to check on arrival.

**Values above are structural, not final.** Real figures are set per blank per size in
Phase 04, and the specification must be *per size* — a fixed elevation on an XS and a 3XL
lands in visibly different places on the body.

## 4.4 Layer three — narrative coherence: one statement, four registers

Each garment carries a different register of the same statement. Together they read as a
set; individually each is complete and none is a fragment.

| Garment | Register | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| **T-shirt** | **The house** | The wordmark. Entry to the brand — it says who |
| **Hoodie** | **The chapter** | The chapter designation. The statement piece |
| **Sweatpants** | **The mark** | The monogram only. The quietest garment in the range |
| **Cap** | **The compact mark** | The monogram, embroidered |

Read as a whole: *house · chapter · mark · mark.* Nobody has to own all four to understand
it, and nobody who owns two feels they are missing a punchline.

## 4.5 Tier assignment

From the locked collection architecture: **THE STANDARD** is permanent, **CHAPTER PRODUCT**
retires when the chapter closes.

| Garment | Tier | Code | On chapter close |
|---|---|---|---|
| T-shirt | Chapter | `GR–001–TS` | Retired permanently |
| Hoodie | Chapter | `GR–001–HD` | Retired permanently |
| Sweatpants | Chapter | `GR–001–SP` | Retired permanently |
| **Cap** | **Standard** | `GR–000–CP` | **Remains. Never retired** |

The cap is deliberately permanent. It is the lowest-price entry to the brand and the
best-margin article; making it chapter-bound would mean rebuilding the entry point every
release. It also gives customers something to return to between chapters, which is what
the Standard tier exists for.

# 5. THE FOUR-PRODUCT SYSTEM

> Requirements, not blank selections. Every line is written to be tested in Phase 04.

## 5.1 `GR–001–TS` — Heavyweight T-Shirt · THE HERO

**Why it exists.** Highest volume, lowest entry price, most wearable outside the gym, and
the product most people will judge the brand by. If this one is mediocre, nothing else
matters.

| | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Weight** | 220 GSM minimum; 240–260 preferred `[A]` |
| **Fibre** | 100% cotton, combed and ring-spun. No blends in this chapter |
| **Block** | Boxy through the body, dropped shoulder, relaxed but not tent-like |
| **Collar** | Ribbed, minimum 2 cm, must recover after wash. **A collapsed collar fails the garment** |
| **Finish** | Pre-shrunk or dimensionally stable; shrinkage under 5% after three 30°C washes |
| **Sizes** | XS–3XL if available; XS mandatory |
| **Colours** | Reign Black, Bone |

**Front:** wordmark, left chest, 50 mm wide, at the specified elevation. Nothing else.

**Back:** the datum rule spanning shoulder-blade width, with `CHAPTER 001 — ASCENSION` set
in mono beneath it. **Total height under 60 mm.** It reads at conversation distance, not
across a room.

That back placement is the chapter's one concession to a graphic, and its size is the
entire point — it is the opposite of a slogan tee. Someone standing next to you can read
it; someone across the gym sees a plain black shirt.

**Two prominent external expressions: front wordmark, back chapter block.** At the limit.
Nothing further on the outside.

## 5.2 `GR–001–HD` — Heavyweight Hoodie · THE STATEMENT

**Why it exists.** Highest contribution per unit, the garment that makes the brand feel
real in the hand, and half of the set.

| | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Weight** | 320 GSM minimum; 350–400 preferred `[A]` |
| **Fibre** | Cotton-dominant. Brushed back |
| **Block** | Boxy, dropped shoulder, matching the tee's silhouette logic |
| **Construction** | Ribbed cuffs and hem with genuine recovery; **flat drawcord, no branded aglets or contrast tipping** |
| **Hood** | Structured enough to stand. A collapsing hood reads cheap immediately |
| **Colours** | Reign Black only — see §9 |

**Front:** wordmark, left chest, 55 mm.
**Back:** the datum rule with `CHAPTER 001 — ASCENSION` beneath, under 70 mm total.
**Detail:** `BEGIN AT GROUND LEVEL.` inside the hood, tonal black-on-black.

That tonal interior line is the chapter's best single detail: invisible in wear, found
only by the owner, and produced with a technique that costs nothing extra.

## 5.3 `GR–001–SP` — Heavyweight Sweatpants · THE QUIET ONE

**Why it exists.** Completes the set, and the set is the highest-value basket in the range.

| | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Weight** | Must match the hoodie's weight class. **Same fabric family, ideally same supplier** |
| **Fibre** | Matched to the hoodie |
| **Block** | Relaxed straight or tapered. Not skinny, not flared |
| **Construction** | Ribbed cuff matching the hoodie's rib; flat drawcord matching the hoodie's; side seam pockets minimum |
| **Colours** | Reign Black only |

**Single external expression: the monogram, upper-left thigh, 40 mm.** Nothing else on
the outside.

Sweatpants should not talk. The hoodie carries the chapter; the pants carry the mark. This
also means that when the set is worn together, the wearer displays **one chapter statement,
not two competing ones.**

## 5.4 `GR–000–CP` — Embroidered Cap · THE ENTRY

**Why it exists.** Three jobs, and they compound: it moves average order value, embroidery
is the strongest anti-POD signal we have, and it is the lowest-risk first purchase from a
house nobody has heard of yet.

| | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Profile** | Low or mid, unstructured or lightly structured. **No high-crown trucker forms** |
| **Fabric** | Matte cotton twill or brushed cotton. No shine, no technical face |
| **Closure** | Metal or webbing, **unbranded**. No plastic snapback unless it is the only option |
| **Stitching** | Tonal throughout. **No contrast stitching anywhere** |
| **Eyelets** | Tonal, or none |
| **Colours** | Reign Black |

**Front:** GR monogram, embroidered, 45 mm.
**Rear:** datum device — solid rule, no notch at this size.

**Embroidery is mandatory on this article.** A printed cap defeats the entire reason the
cap is in the collection.

# 6. PRODUCT NAMING

## 6.1 The recommendation

> **Products are named for what they are. The chapter is carried by the code and the
> label, never by an invented product name.**

**Revision 2 — interim naming, not locked.** The founder has set a restrained house form
for now, to be revisited after product validation:

| Displayed as | Code |
|---|---|
| **THE TEE** · Chapter 001 | `GR–001–TS` |
| **THE HOODIE** · Chapter 001 | `GR–001–HD` |
| **THE CAP** · Chapter 001 | `GR–000–CP` |

*Withdrawn from Chapter 001:* `GR–001–SP` sweatpants and `GR–001–SET`. Specification
archived, not deleted.

**This naming architecture is explicitly not permanent.** The reasoning below for plain,
descriptive naming still stands and is the alternative to decide between after samples.

## 6.2 Why plain names are the correct choice here

While the category names a hoodie *APEX PREDATOR OVERSIZED*, we call it a heavyweight
hoodie. That is not a failure of imagination — it is the naming equivalent of publishing
GSM instead of adjectives, and it is the same differentiator applied to language.

It also solves four practical problems at once: the names are **globally legible** with no
translation risk and no cultural reference; they are **searchable**, because people search
for "heavyweight hoodie" and never for an invented word; they **scale**, since no new
adjective is needed per product per chapter; and they **cannot age badly**, which invented
hype names reliably do.

**The one named product is the set**, because a set needs a name to be sold as a single
thing. `THE 001 SET` is a designation, not a hype name.

**Tradeoff, stated honestly:** plain names give up the small distinctiveness a memorable
product name can earn. That is a real cost. It is worth paying, because a brand claiming
restraint that names its products theatrically is contradicting itself on the product page
— the single place the claim is most easily checked.

# 7. GRAPHIC LANGUAGE

Chapter 001 draws only from the house set. Complete permitted inventory:

- The datum rule
- The `GYMREIGN` wordmark
- The GR monogram
- `CHAPTER 001 — ASCENSION` — mono, +0.2em, uppercase
- `BEGIN AT GROUND LEVEL.` — used sparingly
- `ELEVATION 000` — internal only
- Article codes
- Orthogonal rules

**Not permitted, in this chapter or any other:** illustration of any kind · pictorial
marks · figure drawing · texture or distress · gradients · outline or stroked type ·
diagonals outside letterforms · any second typeface · any accent colour in this chapter.

## Print-method consequence

Every element above is flat, single-colour, hard-edged type or rule. That is deliberate,
and Phase 04 should treat it as a **selection advantage**: it is the easiest artwork class
for DTG to reproduce consistently, the most durable through washing, and the least likely
to fail quality inspection across production runs. Where a provider offers screen print or
DTF for flat single-colour work, that should be evaluated too — this artwork suits those
methods particularly well.

## 8.1 The governing rule, as amended at Phase 02 approval

> **Maximum two prominent external brand expressions per garment. Internal labels and
> legally required garment information are excluded.**

## 8.2 Allocation

| Garment | External expression 1 | External expression 2 | Internal — not counted |
|---|---|---|---|

## 8.3 Placement principles

Every placement measured from the garment hem, specified **per size** · asymmetric
placement preferred over centred · no placement crosses a seam, a zip or a pocket edge ·
no wrap-around, no sleeve-length type, no leg-length type · nothing on the front of a
hood · no placement that a jacket would cut in half.

# 9. COLOURWAYS

## 9.1 Chapter 001

| Garment | Colours | Variants |
|---|---|---|
| T-shirt | Reign Black · Bone | 2 |
| Hoodie | Reign Black | 1 |
| Sweatpants | Reign Black | 1 |
| Cap | Reign Black | 1 |

**Five sellable colour-variants before sizes. No accent colour in this chapter.**

## 9.2 Why the set is black-only

Matching tone across two different blanks is the chapter's highest execution risk. Matching
it across **two colourways** doubles that risk for no strategic gain — and a bone hoodie
against bone sweatpants is *harder* to match than black, because off-whites diverge
visibly in undertone where blacks diverge mainly in depth.

**Restricting the set to one colour concentrates the risk in a single pass/fail test.**
This is a risk decision that also reads as discipline, which is the best kind.

## 9.3 Colour requirements

- **Reign Black must read as true deep black, not charcoal, in daylight.** A blank whose
  "black" reads dark grey fails, regardless of every other quality
- **Bone must read warm neutral, not yellow, not pink, not grey**
- Black garments across the chapter must match each other closely enough to be worn
  together — including tee with sweatpants, not only the set pair
- **All colour judgement is physical.** Screen values and provider catalogue photography
  are not evidence

# 11. THE MATCHED SET

## 11.1 What makes a set a set

Not that two garments are sold together. **That they were made to be worn together and
survive being seen in daylight.**

Four conditions, all required: same fabric family and weight class · the same black ·
consistent rib and trim · complementary rather than duplicated branding.

## 11.2 The gate

> **SET MATCH GATE — pass/fail, binding, separate from the 80/100 quality score.**
>
> Hoodie and sweatpants ordered **in the same order, in the same colourway**, and judged
> **worn together on a body — not as two flat lays** — in daylight and under 4000K
> artificial light.

Flat-lay comparison is not sufficient and should not be accepted as evidence. Fabric
reflects differently at different drape angles, and the mismatch that matters is the one
visible on a person standing up.

A pair may pass the quality score and fail this gate. That is the point of having two.

## 11.3 If the gate fails

In order of preference:

1. **Re-source both garments from a single supplier** and re-sample
2. **Launch them as separate articles**, never marketed or photographed as a set
3. **Hold the sweatpants for Chapter 002**

**Launching a mismatched set is not among the options.** A black that does not match in
daylight damages both garments and, worse, damages the specific claim the brand has made
about publishing real specifications.

## 11.4 Commercial note

The set is the highest-value basket in the range and by far the strongest support for
paid acquisition economics. **It is worth a second sampling round to get right.** The
contingency reserve in the approved budget exists for precisely this.

# 15. CAMPAIGN CONCEPT

## 15.1 The idea

> **Photograph the line, not the climb.**

Every image in this campaign contains a horizontal — a floor edge, a bench, a step nosing,
a shadow boundary, the bar on the ground — and a subject resting on it. Nothing is
mid-motion. Nothing is straining. The campaign is the moment **before**.

## 15.2 Hero image

A figure seated on the edge of a concrete step, shot from a low, level camera. The step
edge runs the full width of the frame as a hard horizontal at the lower third. The figure
occupies the lower portion, wearing the set. **Two-thirds of the frame above them is empty
concrete wall.** Cold neutral grade, one hard light source from the left, deep held blacks.
No face — the head is cropped or turned away.

Type: `BEGIN AT GROUND LEVEL.` set small at the bottom-left, sitting **on** the step edge —
the datum in the photograph and the datum in the layout are the same line.

## 15.3 Shot list

| 01 | Hero — figure on step edge, set, negative space above | Campaign key image |
| 02 | Loaded bar resting on the floor, shot level | Chapter idea, no garment |

| 04 | Tee back, chapter block at reading distance | Shows the graphic honestly |

## 15.4 Rules

No faces · no motion · no smiling · no gym equipment as decoration · no mirrors · no
geographically identifiable location · no golden hour · no visible brand other than ours ·
one light source per frame unless a second is doing structural work.

## 15.5 Motion

If budget allows a single moving piece: **one continuous static shot, 12–20 seconds, of a
figure sitting on the datum, doing nothing.** No cuts, no music, ambient room sound only.
Type appears at the end.

In a feed built entirely from rapid cuts and shouting, a brand confident enough to hold a
still frame for fifteen seconds is doing something none of its competitors will risk.
