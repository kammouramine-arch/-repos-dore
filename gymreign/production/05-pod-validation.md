# 0. THE HEADLINE — READ THIS FIRST

## 0.1 One finding decides this phase

> **The matched set is the binding constraint, and I cannot confirm it is achievable.**

I verified that a genuinely excellent matched pair **exists as a garment**: Stanley/Stella
make a 350 GSM oversized hoodie and 350 GSM full-length jogging pants in the same
heavyweight collection `[V]`. Same manufacturer, same weight, same fabric family — the best
possible starting point for a set.

**What I could not verify is whether any print-on-demand provider carries the jogger.**
Printful's own sweatpants category is dominated by all-over-print polyester joggers and a
Jerzees fleece jogger `[S]` — not premium heavyweight cotton, and not the Stanley/Stella
Flyer. Provider catalogue pages are JavaScript-rendered and do not expose their product
lists to retrieval, so this needs a logged-in dashboard (§13).

**This is a genuine fork, not a formality.** If the jogger is not in catalogue at the same
provider as the hoodie, the matched set as specified in Phase 03 **cannot be produced on
print-on-demand**, and §14 sets out the four ways forward. I am not going to soften that.

## 0.2 What I can state with confidence

1. **A blank specification target now exists in the real world, not in theory.** The
   Stanley/Stella heavyweight range meets or beats every garment requirement set in
   Phase 03 `[V]`.
2. **Gelato is disqualified for our apparel** — its clothing range is Nike, Champion,
   Under Armour, Sport-Tek `[S]`. Third-party branded blanks cannot carry an own-brand
   premium proposition.
3. **Apliiq is disqualified as primary** — fulfilment is US-based (Los Angeles,
   Philadelphia), shipping internationally from the US `[S]`. That destroys the fulfilment
   proximity pillar for a European launch. Its private-label depth is the best of the four
   and it should be revisited for a future US operation.
4. **Printful is the leading candidate**, conditional on §0.1.
5. **The returns exposure identified in Phase 01 is now confirmed at source.** Printful
   does not accept returns for wrong size, wrong colour, or change of mind — the merchant
   absorbs them. Quality defects and misprints do get free reshipment or refund `[V]`.

## 1.2 Why Printify ranks below Printful for this brand

Printify is a marketplace of independent print providers. **The same SKU ordered in two
regions is produced by two different companies**, on different machines, from separately
sourced stock. For a brand whose entire proposition is that the same black arrives every
time — and which must eventually serve EU, UK and US identically — that is a structural
mismatch, not a detail.

It remains the right answer if catalogue breadth turns out to matter more than
consistency: **its network is the most likely place to find a heavyweight cotton jogger**
if Printful has none. That is exactly the §14 fork.

## 2.2 Recommended blanks — and the reasoning that matters

### Hero T-shirt → **Asher `SATU039`, 280 GSM, oversized**

Phase 03 required ≥220 GSM with 240–260 preferred. Asher is **280 GSM and already
oversized**, so it clears the requirement without needing an oversized *fit* to be faked by
sizing up — which is what most POD "oversized" tees actually are.

*Alternative:* Freestyler `SATU018` at 240 GSM relaxed, if Asher proves too heavy in wear
or is unavailable.

### Hoodie + Sweatpants → **Slammer 2.0 `SASU024` + Flyer `SABU006`, both 350 GSM**

**This pairing is the single most important recommendation in the phase.**

Cooper Dry at 400 GSM is the better *hoodie* in isolation — heavier, more substantial,
verified in black across XXS–3XL. **I am not recommending it, and the reason is the set.**

There is no 400 GSM jogger in the range. Pairing a 400 GSM hoodie with a 350 GSM jogger
puts a visible and tactile weight mismatch at the centre of the collection — exactly the
failure Phase 03's set gate exists to catch. **Slammer 2.0 and Flyer are both 350 GSM, both
from the same manufacturer, both in the same heavyweight collection**, which maximises the
chance of a shared dye standard and a matching black.

> **The best hoodie available is not the right hoodie for this collection.** Choosing the
> matched pair over the heavier single garment is the decision the set architecture
> demands.

*If Cooper Dry is preferred as a standalone:* it can only be launched as a standalone
hoodie, never marketed as part of a set with the Flyer.

### Cap → **not yet specifiable**

Stanley/Stella headwear was not verified. Printful's dad-hat embroidery area is
**10.16 × 4.45 cm** `[S]`, which comfortably accommodates the 45 mm GR monogram. The blank
itself must be chosen in-dashboard against the Phase 03 requirements — matte twill,
unbranded closure, tonal stitching, no high-crown trucker form.

> **No retail price is finalised here, per founder instruction.** The model below is a

## 7.1 The one verified cost input

**Blaster 2.0 `SATU020` on Printful: $21.10 USD** `[V, 2026-08-23]`

Useful as an anchor for the order of magnitude of a Stanley/Stella tee on Printful. **It is

## 7.2 The cost stack — French basis

```
  Blank garment cost                    [U]
+ Decoration — DTG / embroidery         [U]
+ Inside label                          ≈$0.99  [S]
= FULFILMENT COST
+ Shipping to customer                  [U]
= LANDED COST
──────────────────────────────────────────────
  Retail (VAT inclusive)
− VAT                                   France 20%  [V]
= NET REVENUE
− Payment processing                    ~2.5% + €0.25  [A]
− Returns & replacement allowance       8%  [A] — see §7.4
= CONTRIBUTION MARGIN
```

## 7.3 The two French VAT regimes

Both must be modelled until the accountant confirms which applies.

- **Régime A — franchise en base.** No VAT charged out; input VAT not reclaimable.
  Thresholds €85,000 / €93,500 for goods `[S]`.
- **Régime B — VAT-registered.** 20% out, input VAT reclaimable.

**Price as if Régime B from day one.** Treat any franchise-period saving as reserve, never
as margin, so crossing the threshold changes nothing the customer sees.

## 7.4 The returns allowance is now evidence-based, not a guess

Printful does not accept returns for wrong size, wrong colour or change of mind — **the
merchant absorbs the full cost of the garment plus shipping** `[V]`. Defects and misprints
are separately covered by free reshipment or refund `[V]`.

Combined with the French 14-day withdrawal right, which the strategy assumes applies to
POD apparel, **every size-driven return is a total loss of one unit.** The 8% allowance
`[A]` stands, and publishing accurate per-size flat measurements moves from good practice
to direct margin protection.

## 7.5 The gate that survives regardless

> **Retail ≥ 2.8 × landed cost. An article that cannot clear it does not launch.**

**If the combined landed cost of hoodie + jogger breaks the floor, the set does not ship as
a set.** That outcome is acceptable. Shipping a set that loses money is not.

# 12. SAMPLE TESTING PROTOCOL

## 12.1 Scorecard — 100 points

| # | Criterion | Weight | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fabric hand and character | 10 | Handled by three people independently |
| 2 | GSM verified against claim | 8 | Weigh a known area; compare to spec |
| 3 | Fit and silhouette | 10 | Worn, photographed front/side/back |
| 4 | Stitching quality | 7 | Stitch density, straightness, loose threads |
| 5 | Construction — seams, collar, cuffs, waistband | 8 | Inspect and stress each |
| 6 | Colour depth and accuracy | 10 | **Daylight + 4000K.** Black must not read charcoal |
| 7 | Print quality | 10 | Edge sharpness, density, hand-feel, no banding |
| 8 | Embroidery quality | 7 | Stitch density, **G counter not filled**, no puckering |
| 9 | Comfort in wear | 7 | Worn for a full day, including a training session |
| 10 | Wash performance | 10 | Three washes at 30°C, line dried |
| 11 | Shrinkage | 6 | Measured before/after. **Fail above 5%** |
| 12 | Durability | 4 | Abrasion at cuff and hem; print crack check |
| 13 | Placement accuracy | 3 | Measured from hem against the elevation spec |
| **Total** | | **100** | |

**Pass threshold: 80/100, with no single criterion below 60% of its weight.**
Criteria 6, 7 and 10 are weighted heaviest because they are the three failures a customer
photographs.

## 12.2 The set match gate — separate, binding, pass/fail

Judged **worn together on a body**, not as two flat lays. Flat-lay comparison is not
evidence: fabric reflects differently at different drape angles, and the mismatch that
matters is the one visible on a person standing up.

Tested in **daylight, 4000K indoor light, flat lay, worn, side-by-side, and photographed.**

> **A pair may score 90/100 on quality and still fail this gate. Do not rationalise a
> visible mismatch.**

## 12.3 Perceived-quality test

One question, asked of five people who have not been told the brand is print-on-demand:

> *"What would you expect to pay for this?"*

If the median answer is below the intended retail price, the problem is the garment or the
photography — not the pricing.

# 14. RISKS AND ALTERNATIVES

## 14.1 If no provider carries a matching jogger — the fork

Ranked. **Option A is strongly preferred; option D is the honest fallback.**

**A. Both garments from one manufacturer at one provider.** Slammer 2.0 + Flyer if
available; otherwise any single manufacturer offering a heavyweight cotton hoodie and
jogger in one fabric family. *Preserves the set as designed.*

**B. Accept a near-match and test it.** A different manufacturer's jogger at similar
weight, subjected to the set gate. **Only if it passes.** Honest but lower odds.

**C. Sell both, market neither as a set.** Hoodie and jogger launch as separate articles
with no set SKU, no paired photography, no set pricing. *Keeps the range; loses the highest
AOV basket and the CAC headroom that came with it.*

**D. Drop the jogger from Chapter 001.** Return to a long-sleeve or crewneck as the fourth
article, and revisit the set in Chapter 002 with better information.

**Option D is not a failure.** It is what the Phase 01 analysis originally recommended, for
exactly the reason now surfacing. The set was approved on the understanding that the risk
would be managed by a gate — this is that gate operating earlier and more cheaply than
expected, at the catalogue stage rather than after buying samples.
