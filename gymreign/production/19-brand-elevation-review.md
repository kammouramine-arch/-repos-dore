## 2. WHY THE CURRENT BRANDING IS WEAK

1. **There is no wordmark.** The single most important asset a clothing house owns does not
   exist as artwork. Everything currently rests on a two-letter monogram.
2. **"GR" is not distinctive.** Two letters in a geometric grotesque is the most contested
   space in apparel. Nothing about the current construction is ownable.
3. **The one genuinely original idea is invisible.** The datum — *everything is measured from
   one line* — is a real concept most gym brands lack. It appears on no product.
4. **The products read as four black garments with a small mark**, not as a collection. No
   repeated device ties them together.
5. **It photographs as nothing.** At feed scale a 45 mm mark on a black garment disappears.
   No silhouette, no shape, no recognition cue.

## 3–4. PROPOSED DIRECTION AND MARK — "THE PLATE"

The fix is not a new idea; it is **making the existing idea visible**. The datum stops being a
quiet system device and becomes the thing you actually see.

**Proposed primary symbol:** `gymreign/identity/assets/monogram-gr-plate.svg` — the GR bounded
above and below by two full-width rules.

Why it works:
- **Shape before letters.** A dense bounded block is recognisable at a glance and at distance;
  the current mark is only recognisable once read.
- **Makes the datum literal.** The two rules *are* the datum. The concept finally appears on
  the garment.
- **Embroiders well.** Two straight rules, two solid letterforms, no fill-prone fine detail.
- **Scales.** Verified legible at **22 px**; clean reversed on black. Rendered and checked at
  190 / 130 / 70 / 58 / 30 / 22 px.
- **Reads as a stamp, not a logo** — specification-plate language, matching a house built on
  published measurements.

**Rejected on the render:** a version with the shared crossbar extended through and past both
letters. The rule read as a **strikethrough** — the letters looked cancelled. Discarded.

**What this does not fix:** the wordmark still has to be drawn. A plate mark is a secondary
asset; a clothing house is recognised by its name. This needs a type designer and is the one
thing in this project that cannot be done well from here. It should be the next real investment.

hem, and nothing else on the outside.**

| Garment | Mark | Placement | Method |

| TEE | Plate, 45 mm | Left chest, 380 mm from hem | DTG |
| HOODIE | Plate, 50 mm | Left chest, 400 mm from hem | DTG |

position, five times.

A premium sock is knitted, not printed. Every Printify option is a printed polyester merch

**Honest route for both:** vests and socks are the two categories where print-on-demand has no
premium answer. They need a different supply route — knitted-in branding for socks, real
outerwear manufacture for vests — which means minimums and inventory. That is a Chapter 002
conversation, not a Printify one.

## 9. PRICE LADDER

### The error being corrected

The €100 tee came from applying the 2.8× floor to **landed** cost, i.e. product cost plus
€11.62 of EU shipping. Almost no brand prices that way. Charging shipping — or setting a
free-shipping threshold — means the floor applies to **product cost only**.

| | Cost $ | Cost € | 2.8× on landed | 2.8× on product |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| TEE | 28.45 | 24.51 | €100 | **€70** |
| HOODIE | 53.37 | 45.98 | €165 | **€130** |
| JOGGER | 42.88 | 36.94 | €140 | **€105** |
| CAP | 19.14 | 16.49 | €60 | **€45** |
| SHORTS | 25.66 | 22.11 | €105 | **€60** |

Model inputs: FX USD→EUR 0.861542 (live), VAT 20%, payment fees 2.5% + €0.25, returns reserve 8%.

### Three ladders

| Ladder | TEE | HOODIE | JOGGER | SHORTS | CAP | Basket contribution | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| L1 — shipping decoupled | €70 | €130 | €105 | €60 | €45 | €156.80 | Floor-minimum; cap and shorts dip just under |
| L2 — entry-led | €65 | €135 | €105 | €55 | €40 | €149.39 | Cheapest entry; four articles under floor |
| **L3 — balanced (RECOMMENDED)** | **€75** | **€135** | **€110** | **€60** | **€45** | **€167.93** | **Recommended** |
| Current live (shipping absorbed) | €92 | €168 | €141 | — | €54 | €170.94 | Reference |

L3 drops the tee from €92 to €75 and holds essentially the same basket contribution —
€167.93 against €170.94, a €3 difference for prices roughly 18% lower.

### L3 unit economics (shipping charged)

| | Price | Contribution | % | Multiple on product cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| TEE | €75 | €30.86 | 49.4% | 3.06× |
| HOODIE | €135 | €53.89 | 47.9% | 2.94× |
| JOGGER | €110 | €44.39 | 48.4% | 2.98× |
| SHORTS | €60 | €22.14 | 44.3% | 2.71× |
| CAP | €45 | €16.64 | 44.4% | 2.73× |

Cap and shorts sit a hair under the 2.8× floor at 2.71–2.73×. **This is a deliberate accepted
exception**, to be recorded in the assumptions register: both are basket-builders whose job is
to lift order value, and both still clear 44% contribution.

### Shipping model that makes L3 work

**Free shipping over €120 · flat €9.90 below.** The threshold sits just above the jogger and
below the hoodie, so a single hoodie ships free and any two-item basket clears it — which is
the 2-unit basket the acquisition model assumes.
