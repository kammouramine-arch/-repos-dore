# 3. CUSTOMS, VAT AND THE CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE

Verified against the European Commission's own guidance, not inferred.

## 3.1 The rule that matters most — and it is good news

> **The €3 temporary customs duty applies per item, from 1 July 2026 to 1 July 2028, on
> consignments up to €150 — and the declarant (seller, IOSS holder or indirect
> representative) is liable, not the consumer.** `[V — European Commission]`
>
> *"Consumers are liable only in exceptional cases where member states offer free web-based
> declaration systems."*

**This is already in force** — it began 1 July 2026, seven weeks ago. It replaced the old
€150 duty exemption.

**Combined with IOSS, which continues to operate separately for import VAT** `[V]`, a
correctly configured setup means:

> **The customer pays exactly what they see at checkout, and receives no bill on delivery.**

That is the outcome the brand strategy demanded, and it is achievable.

## 3.2 The €150 line runs straight through our price ladder

| Order | Value | Treatment | Cost to us |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tee | €65 | ≤€150 · IOSS covers VAT · €3 duty | €3.00 — **4.6%** of retail |
| Jogger | €105 | ≤€150 · IOSS · €3 duty | €3.00 — **2.9%** |
| Hoodie | €140 | ≤€150 · IOSS · €3 duty | €3.00 — **2.1%** |
| Tee + cap | €105 | ≤€150 · IOSS · €3 **per item** = €6 | €6.00 — **5.7%** |
| **Hoodie + jogger** | **€245** | **OVER €150 · IOSS does not apply** | **Standard duty ≈12% of customs value** `[A]` |

**Two consequences worth understanding:**

1. **The duty is per item, not per parcel.** A four-item order under €150 carries €12 of
   duty. That penalises exactly the multi-item baskets the AOV strategy depends on.
2. **Orders above €150 leave the simplified regime entirely.** Duty is charged on *customs
   value* — roughly what we pay for the goods, not retail — so ≈12% of ~€70 on a
   hoodie-and-jogger order is ≈€8, or about 3% of retail. **Manageable, but only if we ship
   DDP.**

## 3.3 The condition that makes or breaks it

> **Ship Delivered Duty Paid (DDP). Never DAP.**

Under DAP the carrier collects duty and import VAT from the customer at the door, **plus a
handling fee typically larger than the duty itself**. That is the surprise-bill experience
the strategy identified as a structural weakness in US competitors — and it would be
self-inflicted here.

Under DDP we pay, the customer sees nothing, and the cost sits in the margin model where it
belongs — at roughly **2–6% of retail**, which the 2.8× floor absorbs.

## 3.4 Three conditions before this is truly viable

| # | Condition | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **IOSS registration** for the French entity, or use of an intermediary | Accountant |
| 2 | **Confirm Inkthreadable ships DDP** and accepts an IOSS number — they document IOSS support, but DDP for >€150 consignments is unconfirmed `[U]` | Inkthreadable |
| 3 | **Confirm the apparel duty rate and whether UK–EU preferential origin applies.** These garments are made in Asia, so TCA preference probably fails — the €3 regime explicitly excludes goods under preferential agreements `[A]` | Accountant / customs broker |

**None of these is a blocker. All three are setup tasks.** But none should be assumed done.
