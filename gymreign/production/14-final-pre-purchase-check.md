# 1. THE FINDING YOU NEED BEFORE YOU APPROVE

> ## DDP is NOT available. Inkthreadable ships DAP.

Their own Brexit guidance, verbatim `[V]`:

> *"Inkthreadable trades on a **DAP (Delivered At Place)** basis with our customers; this is
> how most goods will be exported to the EU."*

**Under DAP the end customer bears import VAT and customs duties.** Their guidance tells
merchants to *"inform customers during checkout that they may owe import fees upon
delivery."*

**That is the surprise-bill experience the brand strategy identified as a structural
weakness in US competitors — and here we would be inflicting it on ourselves.**

## 1.1 IOSS mitigates it, but only partly

A merchant can submit **their own IOSS number** `[V]`, which eliminates import VAT at
delivery. But:

- **IOSS applies only to consignments ≤ €150** `[V — European Commission]`
- **Our own price ladder crosses that line.** A hoodie-and-jogger order at €245 sits above
  it, and gets no IOSS protection at all
- Inkthreadable's IOSS article mentions **no €150 limit and never mentions DDP or DAP** —
  merchants applying it could easily assume broader cover than it gives

## 1.2 What an EU customer actually experiences

| Order | Value | With merchant IOSS | Customer experience |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tee | €65 | ≤€150 → VAT collected at checkout | ✅ Clean. Duty position under DAP needs confirming `[U]` |
| Jogger | €105 | ≤€150 → covered | ✅ Clean |
| Hoodie | €140 | ≤€150 → covered | ✅ Clean |
| **Hoodie + jogger** | **€245** | **Above €150 — no cover** | ❌ **Import VAT ~€49 + duty + carrier handling on the doorstep** |

**The €150 line runs straight through the middle of the collection**, and it penalises
exactly the multi-item basket the AOV strategy depends on.

## 4.2 A legitimate optimisation worth considering

**Split into two consignments, each under €150**, and apply IOSS:

| Consignment | Contents | Value |
|---|---|---|
| A | 2 × hoodie | ≈ **€104** ✅ |
| B | 2 × tee + 2 × jogger + 2 × cap | ≈ **€115** ✅ |

item — €18 across six items** rather than ~€30 ad valorem. Cost: a second shipping charge,
and IOSS registration must already be in place.

**This is also a preview of a real commercial question:** if splitting consignments is how we
avoid punishing customers, that has to be designed into fulfilment — not discovered later.

# 5. MY ASSESSMENT

**The garments are right. The fulfilment terms are the problem.**

Everything about the product held up: the jogger is 440 GSM oversized in Deep Black with XS
sizing, the tee and hoodie share Black C002, all four pieces ship from one provider, and
embroidery is available where it matters.

**But DAP with a 6–21 day standard service is not a premium customer experience**, and the
€150 IOSS ceiling cuts straight through the price ladder. **For the sample order this is
merely a cost. For the business it is a structural issue that needs answering before
launch** — not before this purchase, but before the first customer order.

**Three ways forward, and this is your call:**

1. **Approve the samples anyway.** ≈€341 landed, inside the tranche. The garments are the
   thing being tested, and the fulfilment question can be solved separately. **This is what I
   would do** — the sourcing conclusion does not depend on the shipping terms.
2. **Approve the samples split into two sub-€150 consignments** — cheaper on duty, tests the
   IOSS mechanism in practice, needs IOSS registration first.
3. **Pause and resolve fulfilment terms first** — ask Inkthreadable directly whether any DDP
   arrangement exists for business accounts, and price the alternative before committing.
